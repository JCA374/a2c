Curriculum

Every run's stages: the world, the reward, the check that ends the stage, the cap and the expected outcome, written down before the stage starts.

Why two stages

  • Learn the road: a beginner spends most of a 1000-frame episode on the grass, learning nothing new. So this stage cuts an episode short after 1 s with all four wheels on grass, or 3 s without reaching a new tile, at a price of −50, so that ending early is never a shortcut.
  • Race: once the car keeps to the road, training switches to the task's own rules (1000 frames, no early stops), so it also learns to recover from mistakes instead of having them cut off.
  • The reward is CarRacing's own score in both stages. The agent is trained on exactly what it is judged on.
  • Stages switch on the validation mean (16 fixed tracks, deterministic). Checks never use training episodes, which are noisy and drive a random policy.
