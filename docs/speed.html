The lidar bake-off

The new lidar casts 19 rays, sampling every metre up to 60 m and testing each sample against the nearby road tiles. Six implementations of the same kernel (experiments/bakeoff/), each checked against the pure-Python reference on 900 car poses × 19 rays on three real tracks:

The choice: numba

All six give the same answers bit for bit. C++ with -O3 -march=native is the fastest on a batch, but an env step casts one pose at a time, where numba's call is cheapest. Its source is the Python reference itself, so the two cannot drift apart, and it needs no build step. A full step now costs about 5× the physics floor; the rest is Python glue and the vector-env pipes. The next speed-up would be to move the whole observation into numba. It isn't worth doing yet: the update and the eval workers share the same 8 threads.
